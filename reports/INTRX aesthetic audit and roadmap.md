# Light the stage, lift the rules

INTRX's 520 components are functionally complete and fall short of production grade for a reason both visual audits reach independently: they are well-engineered objects shown small, grey and half-empty on a flat stage, in a design the Figma file itself specifies and the repo's rules currently forbid changing. Reconciled across all 520 posters and 300 live detail views, **138 components (27%) can ship as they are, 254 (49%) are one shared presentation pass away, 110 (21%) need individual redesign and 18 (3%) should be cut or merged**. The photo complaint is a change of art direction, not an implementation miss: the shipped photos match Figma's "dark editorial" library almost pixel for pixel, **only 2 of 24 assets meet the new dark-abstract brief**, and a same-key swap of 48 files re-skins all 119 photo-using components. The 28 November handoff leaves seven acceptance areas open and none is a functional break; the real cost driver is a verification regime (334 verifier scripts, 350 MB of fixtures, a 2,620-line AGENTS.md) that turned one-line fixes into hour-long ceremonies and rejected the only optimisation that fixed the one failing frame-rate case. The shortest path is a one-page owner decision record that waives three unfinished numeric gates, retires the byte-exact evidence regime and lifts five standing rules, followed by deployment, a shared stage-and-content pass and the photo swap: **roughly two working weeks to a credible public launch and three to four to benchmark-tier polish**, on estimates nobody has tested. Growth should come from page sections, shader backgrounds and agent-shell pieces rather than more one-off effects, organised into seven intent-first groups; code can be ported from MIT and Apache-2.0 sources (Magic UI, Paper Shaders, Cult UI, Kokonut, Motion Primitives and others) but not from React Bits, Aceternity, transitions.dev or Animate UI, whose terms exclude exactly the kind of redistributed catalog INTRX is. Three cautions frame everything below: no replacement image has been viewed, licence clauses are paraphrased and must be re-read before acting, and every effort figure is an estimate.

## Ten ordered steps and fifteen owner decisions stand between INTRX and launch

The sequence below follows one logic. Decisions come first because every later step collides with a standing rule. Deployment comes early because real-network numbers replace three open-ended local audits. Shared fixes precede individual redesign because 254 components move on shared changes alone. The photo swap runs in parallel because owner curation, not agent time, is its bottleneck. The extension lane waits until a licence policy exists. Steps 0 to 4 plus step 8 make the site launchable; steps 5 to 7 lift it to the level of the libraries the owner reposts; step 9 is a rolling backlog. The handoff research budgets its own narrower version of this plan at about 5.5 to 8.5 agent-assisted days, assuming at most 300 restyles and 10 to 20 ports ([handoff note](../research_notes/INTRX%20aesthetic%20audit%20and%20roadmap/handoff_outstanding_work.md)); both visual audits independently put the shared stage work alone at about a week, which is why the composite here is longer.

## One picture of 520: 138 ship, 254 share a fix, 110 need redesign

The two visual audits cover complementary halves. One viewed all 261 posters and 157 live detail views across twelve product-UI categories plus the site shell ([product-UI audit](../research_notes/INTRX%20aesthetic%20audit%20and%20roadmap/visual_audit_product_ui.md)); the other viewed all 259 posters and 143 detail routes across fourteen effects and expressive categories ([effects audit](../research_notes/INTRX%20aesthetic%20audit%20and%20roadmap/visual_audit_effects.md)). Added together they give the first catalog-wide classification, and they agree on the diagnosis even where their remedies differ.

### The Figma tiles, not the build, set the ceiling

Both auditors checked implementation against design and reached the same verdict: all 19 sampled posters (nine product-UI, ten effects) reproduce their Figma v4 tiles essentially one for one, placeholder bars and sparse pictograms included ([Figma file](https://www.figma.com/design/OpP74UYjFHRU5q9OBPhoSY/Interaction?node-id=0-1)). The repo rule that the v4 tile overrides older art enforced this fidelity, and per-family verifiers pin exact native layers and SVG bytes ([AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md)). Polishing fidelity further cannot change the owner's complaint. Two structural facts compound it. Every component is two different designs: the thumbnail is the Figma stencil, while the detail view is an older composition with different art, data and sometimes a different resting state. And the detail view never scales up, because the Stage clamps fit scale to at most 1 ([stage.js](../js/shell/stage.js)), so a 400×320 composition covers 23% of the 986×560 stage and a 260×100 control sits alone in it. The result is a detail page that is often less impressive than its own thumbnail.

The site shell grades B+: credible Vercel-and-Linear-style chrome and a strong command palette, held back by a hero with no live showpiece above the fold, outline-only tile cards, a code panel that opens on boilerplate and visible light-theme defects ([product-UI audit](../research_notes/INTRX%20aesthetic%20audit%20and%20roadmap/visual_audit_product_ui.md)). ### Six root causes repeat across both halves

The first is the Stage itself. The dark Stage is a flat `#151515`, the same value as the page canvas, with cards at `#1C1C1C` giving about 1.07:1 separation; only the light Stage has a lighting asset, and tile cards are transparent with no elevation or hover lift ([foundations.css](../css/foundations.css)). Add the fit clamp, a Dock band that pushes subjects 26 to 28 px above centre while hidden, and a 48 px play disc drawn over the subject of every non-live tile, and the frame works against its contents. Practitioner guidance explains why this reads as dead: on dark interfaces depth comes from surfaces that lighten as they approach the viewer, translucent hairlines with a top highlight, and layered rather than single shadows ([Superhuman](https://blog.superhuman.com/how-to-design-delightful-dark-themes/), [Vercel guidelines](https://vercel.com/design/guidelines), [Josh Comeau](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/css/designing-shadows/)).

The second is emptiness. About 73 of the 261 product-UI tiles use grey bars where content should be; on the effects side 127 of 259 posters contain no saturated pixel at all, and 35 of the 207 dark-stage tiles carry under 3% ink ([posters](../assets/posters/)). The third is the two-designs problem already described, which also means many full views open at 0%, empty or collapsed while their tiles show the hero state. The fourth is lab chrome and build-process copy inside compositions: verifier-style captions, "stays in this demo" disclaimers in at least nine demos, kanban cards describing the repo's own verification work, component names repeated as in-stage headings, DRAMS spec paragraphs, native `<select>` elements in 20 effect demos, and units such as "Utc+6" and "128.4 Mhz" in the FUI set.

The fifth is the absence of a shared material. The 261 product modules contain roughly 7,248 hex literals against 123 colour-token references, with eleven near-identical greys, seven hairline alphas and more than twenty radii in common use; effect demos average close to zero token references, none of the 259 assigns a Canvas glow and only ten use additive blending, and Skeuomorph demos average 33 unique hexes each with no cross-family light model ([registry](../registry/index.json)). The sixth is a typographic seam: the shell is Geist, every product demo and about 160 effect demos declare Inter, 13 demos request a JetBrains Mono face that is not shipped, declared sizes of 8 to 10 px are common, and `text-wrap` is never used ([fonts.css](../css/fonts.css)). The craft research adds two shell-level observations: secondary and tertiary text (`#A0A0A0` at about 7.0:1 and `#949494` at about 6.0:1, computed) are almost the same colour, so hierarchy rests on size alone, and shell labels at 8 to 10 px break the repo's own 11 px floor ([craft-standards note](../research_notes/INTRX%20aesthetic%20audit%20and%20roadmap/polish_craft_standards.md)).

### The weakest and strongest components already describe the fix

The weakest product-UI items are either placeholders standing in for the thing demonstrated or full views with rendering defects: `agent-prompt-composer` (a raw native file input), `logo-marquee` (dots and bars instead of logos), `map-topo-extrude` (a flat grey grid), `command-palette`, `ticker-tape-prices` (split digits), `bento-spotlight` (a spotlight at 6% white) and `btn-border-beam`, whose beam is nearly invisible. The weakest effects are blank at rest or unlit: `fx-meteors` (empty in two of three idle frames), `fx-beam-grid`, `gradient-mesh-drift` (a mesh gradient rendered grey in a hard-edged box), `cursor-particle-emitter` (five grey dots), `click-fireworks`, `zippo-flame`, `lava-lamp` and `webgl-wobbly-sphere`. Many of these are name-alikes of famous Aceternity, React Bits and Paper effects, so visitors will compare them directly.

The strongest show what the house style can reach without abandoning restraint: `form-date-range`, `agent-diff-review`, `data-line-tooltip`, `text-ransom-note`, `scroll-color-theme` and `btn-polish-stages` on one side; `fx-black-hole`, `glass-refraction-card`, `fx-liquid-glass`, the dither and halftone set, and the DRAMS devices on the other. They share specific believable content, a subject that fills at least half the frame, one real depth cue and an accent used as light or as a single precise mark. The effects auditor estimates only 45 to 60 of the 259 would survive as a social card today; the product auditor shortlists about 15 of 261 with hero appeal.

### Ten shared fixes carry most of the gain

Merged and ordered by impact over effort, the two audits and the craft research converge on the following. Stage presentation comes first: remove the fit clamp so compositions fill the detail Stage, give the dark Stage a key light, floor falloff and static grain, separate it from the page colour, centre previews when the Dock is hidden, restore the single trigger pill the Figma tiles show and move the play disc to a corner. Hero state by default comes second: detail views open in the tile's best state and play once, posters are captured at the effect's peak frame, and each component resolves to one composition. Third is a defect sweep of the eleven product-UI defects that are bugs rather than taste, plus the clipped "Approv" label, the cropped Command Palette tile and the invisible light-theme toolbar icon. Fourth is copy and chrome hygiene: move titles, instructions, readouts and selects out of the composition into the detail rail. Fifth is a surface recipe and elevation ladder applied by codemod, collapsing the stray greys, alphas and radii onto tokens and lifting secondary text to about `#B4B4B4`. Sixth is typography: Geist for demo UI, Geist Mono for code and instruments, an 11 px floor. Seventh is a shared light recipe for canvas effects (glow sprite, additive pass, persistence trails, vignette) with ambient backgrounds made full-bleed; only 12 of the 259 effects are bleed today. Eighth is a material kit and one light direction for Skeuomorph and DRAMS, with Skeuomorph defaulting to the light Stage where its materials were authored. Ninth is real content for skeleton tiles, with tiles for Navigation and Scroll derived from their stronger full views and a small licensed kit of wordmark logos, app icons, avatars and a vector world outline. Tenth is curation: a featured set, the 18 cut-or-merge items hidden, cluster members shown as variants, and a showcase band on Home.

The audits name the clusters that make the grid look padded: nine near-duplicate groups in product UI (two "Wavelength" text stills, radial and bloom menus, three stepper-dot components, three checklists) and, in effects, pointer-reactive dot grids, line fields, grey blobs, eight ASCII demos, seven scanline effects and 32 DRAMS sliders and toggles. The effects auditor judges that roughly 190 to 200 parents with variants behind a switcher would look stronger than 259 lookalikes, and that the "520" headline survives if variants are counted.

### Where the audits and the craft research pull apart

Four disagreements need an owner view rather than more research. On fit, the product-UI auditor wants small controls auto-fitted to about 45–60% of the detail Stage while the effects auditor targets about 80% and full bleed for ambient pieces; the sensible reading is that the target depends on role. On the Stage, the craft research keeps `#151515` and adds light, whereas the product-UI auditor would darken the Stage to about `#0C0C0C` or lift cards; both are compatible with lighting, but only one base value can ship. On colour, the product-UI auditor says add no hues and remove the stray violet and blue, the reposts profile shows the owner consistently endorsing monochrome plus one orange accent, and the effects auditor argues the blanket rule removes the reason Ambient, Liquid, 3D, Physics and particle effects exist and proposes an accent ramp plus one cool counter-hue. The evidence supports the ramp (accent used as light is what the strongest effects already do); the counter-hue rests on one auditor's judgement. On motion, the audits say leave timing alone while the craft research lists small deviations (a weak built-in `ease-in-out`, entrances from scale 0.25 and 0.7, 400–500 ms swaps); these are defensible refinements and should wait.

One uncomfortable observation belongs here. A second-hand report says Anthropic's rewritten frontend-design guidance names near-black with a single vivid accent as one of three looks AI output clusters around ([dev.to](https://dev.to/kenimo49/anthropic-rewrote-frontend-design-skill-3-ai-design-cliches-named-with-hex-codes-22m4)); the researcher could not verify it against the skill file. If true, INTRX's palette is not the differentiator, and distinction has to come from what defaults lack: light, material, authored type, realistic content and one consistent image world.

## Photography: 22 of 24 assets miss the brief, and one swap re-skins 119 components

### The gap is against the owner's new intent, not against Figma

INTRX ships 24 credited Unsplash images, each as 1600 px and 800 px WebP under the free Unsplash License, used by 119 components through 338 component-photo links and 824 references in 115 source modules ([photo-credits.json](../assets/photo-credits.json), [registry](../registry/index.json)). Measured and viewed, **only silk and fire are dark, abstract and neutral-or-warm**; 14 are plainly representational (forests, night cities, portraits, aurora, Milky Way, flowers, an interior) and 11 are blue, teal, green or violet dominant. Usage makes it worse: the eight most-used assets (ocean in 42 components, archGrid 34, forestDark 32, cityNight 31, cityTeal 29, forestBlue 28, dusk 23, aurora 20) are the least abstract, truly abstract images account for 8% of links, and cool-hued photos for 62%. On posters the photography is consistently dark, so nothing looks broken, but it supplies a second and third hue beside an orange-accent system that forbids demos from doing the same, and the same five images repeat across about ten gallery tiles ([photo note](../research_notes/INTRX%20aesthetic%20audit%20and%20roadmap/photo_imagery_gap.md)).

Against the design, there is no gap. The Figma file holds exactly one photo library, frame 104:4, titled "Photo library v2 — dark editorial (Unsplash)", and every sampled tile shows the same forests, cities, ocean and portraits that shipped ([Figma 104:4](https://www.figma.com/design/OpP74UYjFHRU5q9OBPhoSY/Interaction?node-id=104-4)). Handoff 3 asked for real Unsplash photographs from that set only and forbade gradients posing as photos ([CODEX_HANDOFF_3.md](../CODEX_HANDOFF_3.md)); the implementer recovered each original and recorded RGB differences of 0.00 to 2.27 against the Figma rasters ([ASSET_ART_DIRECTION.md](../ASSET_ART_DIRECTION.md)). Only six of the 30 library swatches are abstract. The fair framing is that the specification said dark editorial, the build matched it, and the owner has now set a different direction and has confirmed that the Figma imagery is not the target either. That direction has to be written down as an owner instruction, because it contradicts "the tile wins", "approved v2 photography" and the many rules that pin exact photo slots and hashes. One housekeeping point follows from the owner's exclusion of generated imagery: three retired generated images still sit in `assets/images` (about 7.7 MB of PNG); no component lists them, but two raster modules still name them and whether anything loads them was not traced.

### A proposed series: matter and light, neutral first, amber only

The photo research proposes replacing the bytes behind all 24 keys with one art-directed series, working title "Matter & Light", in six families: graphite and bronze 3D forms, pigment pours, light drawn on black, smoke and ember, dark matter textures (sand, cloth, sheet) and sculptural busts for person slots. Near-black neutrals carry at least two thirds of the set; warm images use only amber, bronze and ember hues at restrained saturation so the UI accent stays the most saturated thing on a tile; subjects are scale-free with no horizon so any crop reads; recognisable places, people, nature tropes and anything labelled AI are out. A single treatment unifies mixed sources: crop to each key's existing aspect ratio, hold mean luma to about 15–35 of 255 with highlights capped, neutralise cool casts, bake in light grain and a gentle vignette, and draw one consistent inner hairline on every slot in CSS. The numeric targets are derived from the current photos that sit well and are proposals to tune by eye.

Thirty-three candidates were opened on Unsplash and each page showed the free licence; none is Unsplash+. Unsplash's licence permits free commercial use without permission, forbids selling images without significant modification and forbids compiling them into a competing service ([Unsplash License](https://unsplash.com/license)), and its submission guidelines accept only original photos and 3D renders and reject AI-generated content ([Unsplash guidelines](https://help.unsplash.com/en/articles/2534415-unsplash-submission-guidelines)). The mapping keeps every key as a slot id so no component reference changes.

The other notes complicate the proposal in four ways. The craft research and the reposts research both suggest procedural gradients or in-house shader imagery for photo slots; the owner has excluded generated assets, so those suggestions are void. The reposts research finds the owner's endorsed imagery is textured (dither, halftone, particles, fluid smoke) more than smooth, which favours the pours, streaks and matter textures over the glossy render family. The product-UI auditor wants the gallery pool widened to 20–30 images and the effects auditor wants it doubled, whereas the same-key plan holds at 24; the cheap sequence is to swap first and add keys later. And the effects auditor found that in effect demos the commoner fault is under-exposure (photos at mean luma 21–29 disappearing into the Stage so the effect cannot be read), which sits inside the proposed 15–35 target; images that carry zoom, Ken Burns, interlace or lens effects need more mid-tone detail than gallery backdrops, so exposure should be set per role on a contact sheet rather than by one rule.

### The swap is hours of work, plus whatever the verification regime demands

The cheapest correct path keeps all 24 keys, file names, widths and per-key aspect ratios: replace 48 files, rewrite `photo-credits.json` (the builder checks licence fields and both SHA-256 values, a safeguard worth keeping), refresh the credit copies embedded in 17 extension modules, then regenerate the asset bundle, registry and 119 posters ([build-registry.js](../tools/build-registry.js)). The mandatory phase checks do not pin photo bytes. The cost sits elsewhere: 40 fixtures hard-code the old hashes, about 151 of 334 verifier scripts touch photos in some way, and raster demos (dither, halftone, ASCII, pixel sort) store model histories computed from the current pixels. Under the current regime the research estimates a few hours for the swap and "several agent-days" for the verification tail; under the slimmed regime the handoff research budgets the whole package at half a day. Owner curation and treatment add half a day to a day either way. Remaining chores are a sweep of subject words in copy, alt text and prompts (up to 111 modules mention forests, oceans, cities and the like), text-contrast repairs that were tuned against the old images, and an optional later rename of keys such as `forestDark` that will no longer describe their contents. A paid tier raises one licence point: Unsplash images may not be sold without significant modification, which matters if Pro ever sells components whose main content is an unmodified photo.

## The handoff left seven acceptance items open; a rules reset closes them faster than more audits

### What the handoff considered unfinished

The handoff states that all 520 components, the routed shell, the registry, the CLI and MCP package and the Pro scaffolding are implemented, and that product acceptance is not ([LLM_HANDOFF](../docs/LLM_HANDOFF_2026-11-28.md)). Its pending table lists seven areas. Aesthetics has no independent sign-off; a delegated review was stopped at the owner's request before any findings. Detail startup has 128 of 4,160 planned measurements, 35 of them over the strict 300 ms target, before the run died on a tracing error, which is itself the second item (profiler reliability). A single historical "Show more" click timed out and was never reproduced. Mobile Lighthouse is unstable: the same shell source scored between 62 and 100 on a machine running other projects. Reactive Threads' native tile measures about 17 to 19 fps at 4× CPU throttle against a 60 fps target. Accessibility has broad scans and many targeted repairs but leaves photographic, non-text and transient-state contrast, native and normal-motion states, and assistive-technology usability unreviewed; the whole-catalog scan recorded 2,462 incomplete rule results across 15,269 nodes ([a11y inventory](../docs/accessibility/catalog-initial-2026-11-26.json)).

None of these is a broken feature. The same scan ran 4,160 cases with zero boot or page errors; warm detail navigation missed the target in 0 of 64 samples, with a worst case of 185 ms; cold stage paint has a median of about 310 ms against an original 2–3 seconds ([startup sample](../docs/performance/catalog-startup-2026-11-28-partial.json)). The handoff research adds four launch items no audit will close: the site is not deployed from current source and its generated URLs disagree about the canonical origin; the npm package `@tanziro/intrx` is unpublished although Home, detail pages and the MCP config already advertise its `npx` command; the README and progress ledger still say work is in progress; and the Pro flag is off by design ([handoff note](../research_notes/INTRX%20aesthetic%20audit%20and%20roadmap/handoff_outstanding_work.md)).

### Building was fast; verifying was the time sink

Git history separates the two. The 121 new and extension components landed in 30.0 hours, about 15 minutes each. The final-audit tail then produced 64 commits in 68.3 hours without closing a gate; a representative commit put one CSS backing on seven captions and touched 11 files, added a verifier, an evidence file and an AGENTS paragraph, and took 66 minutes. The estate that grew around the product is large: 334 `verify-*.js` and 48 `profile-*.js` scripts, 1,164 fixture files at 350 MB, a local evidence store of 34.6 GB with 77 sealed archives, and an AGENTS.md of 2,620 lines (roughly 50,000 tokens loaded into every agent session, by the researcher's estimate). Zero-tolerance pixel and byte oracles turned harmless renderer differences into failures to investigate, and they rejected the GPU renderer that took Reactive Threads to about 60 fps because its alpha bytes were not identical. Gates written for two routes were expanded into 4,160-case matrices that never completed. A fill gate that needs 163 written exceptions, each with a Figma node id, is not doing useful work.

If a github.io site exists it can only show the pre-remediation build.

### How reuse works in a zero-build vanilla repo

No bundler or framework is needed. Two routes cover the use case. The first is an inline port: translate the React, Tailwind and Motion source into prefixed vanilla CSS and JS inside the usual `INTRX.register` object, targeting the `foundations.css` tokens so the design scale is not lost in de-Tailwinding. Most "magic" components are small (Magic UI's Border Beam is 107 lines importing only Motion and a class helper), so the research estimates 5 to 20 minutes to port a CSS-only or self-contained WebGL piece and 20 to 45 minutes to make it INTRX-ready, rising to one to four hours for layout-animation, three.js or accessible-widget ports; none of this was timed.

The second route is a per-demo dependency, never shell-wide. The seam already exists and is unused: `js/shell/registry.js` and the exporter carry a library map for GSAP 3.12.5, ScrollTrigger and Lenis 1.1.14, the exporter injects `libs` into the single-file HTML, the detail page prints the stack, and 0 of 520 items declare any ([registry](../registry/index.json)). Extending it means adding entries (through `export.shared.js`, the documented extension point, since the base exporter is labelled frozen), pinning exact versions, and loading UMD or ESM builds from the CDN or a vendored copy. The notes differ on hosting: the handoff research prefers self-hosting pinned files under `assets/vendor/` so exports work offline, while the landscape research advises loading GSAP from the CDN because its licence reserves all rights. Both hold if permissive libraries are vendored with their notices and GSAP stays on the CDN. A sensible allow-list is Lenis (6 kB), one house animation engine (Motion is MIT; GSAP has the richer plugins and the custom licence), OGL, Paper Shaders pinned exactly because its 0.0.x versions break, cobe, number-flow and canvas-confetti, with three.js, Rive and Lottie opt-in for size. Dependencies erode INTRX's headline differentiator, so ported and dependent demos should carry a visible dependency facet and the dependency-free core should stay the default.

Three attribution artefacts make a port lawful, on the researchers' reading of MIT and Apache terms: a root `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` listing each upstream project, copyright line, licence text and the demo ids derived from it; a `source` or credits record on the registry item, shown on the detail page; and a licence comment at the top of the exported single-file HTML, which matters most because users copy one file and the notice must travel with it. The exporter already preserves licence comments for photos, so the mechanism exists. Checks need matching edits: the offline-export verifiers need a declared exception for `libs` demos, and the `packages/intrx` tests reference `libs` and were not read. Under the current verification regime the landscape research budgets one to two hours per ported component; under the slim regime the port itself dominates.

### Two provenance questions to settle before a paid tier

Ten of the sixteen Interactive Showcase demos cite React Bits pages as their reference (Dot Grid, Threads, Ballpit, Ripple Grid, Magnet Lines, Pixel Trail, Ribbons, Circular Gallery, Card Swap, Elastic Slider) and are documented as independent vanilla interpretations ([SHOWCASE_SOURCES.md](../SHOWCASE_SOURCES.md)). Because React Bits' licence names ported versions explicitly, the owner should confirm none was written from React Bits source and keep that note on file; two of those ten (Ripple Grid and Elastic Slider) are already on the effects auditor's cut list as duplicates. Separately, the 52 DRAMS items are recreations of designs by 1042 Studio, and the repo records no permission ([DRAMS_IMPLEMENTATION.md](../DRAMS_IMPLEMENTATION.md)). Many other ids mirror Aceternity, transitions.dev, Reverse UI and Bencho names; borrowed names are tolerable where the code is original, though a different title is the safer habit. The research is explicit that it is not legal advice and that a lawyer should confirm the Commons Clause reading and the notice plan before Pro launches.

## What is unviewed, estimated or contested

The image evidence is the thinnest. No replacement candidate was seen; brightness, hue, composition and crop suitability are inferred from page text. The treatment targets are extrapolated from four current photos. Whether dithers and halftones read well on a ceramic bust is untested, and the render-free mapping does not exist yet. The Figma comparison rests on one full board, a handful of tiles and the implementer's recorded identity measurements, 23 tiles in all across three researchers.

The audits are stills. Of 520 detail views, 300 were captured; the other 220 were graded from posters and category siblings and could shift a grade either way. Hover, drag and typed states were barely exercised, frame rate could not be judged from headless software rendering, the two graders were not cross-calibrated, and counts such as "73 skeleton tiles" are visual tallies. Size figures in the effects audit are read from half-scale sheets at about ±15%. The evidence images live in a temporary scratchpad and will not survive a reboot; the durable equivalents are the posters and live routes.

The licence matrix is paraphrase. Aceternity's and 21st.dev's wording came from automated page fetches rather than licence files; Skiper's, Codrops' central page and the Motion+ licence could not be read; libraries.dev's MIT claim comes from its marketing page; Paper Shaders is Apache-2.0 in the repository today, but one researcher recalls stricter earlier terms, so the specific package version should be checked. The notes also disagree on facts. The landscape research records transitions.dev's licence as not stated while the reposts research read a custom licence with an anti-competing-library clause from its repository, and the more specific finding should govern. React Bits is counted at about 218 components in one note and 226 in another, Magic UI at 80 registry files or "150+" by its own site, and Originkit at 813 components or 58 functional plus 805 shader-art entries. Component names attributed to Magic UI and Aceternity in the effects audit come partly from memory.

Every effort figure is an estimate. Per-component times are derived from commit intervals, which cannot separate agent thinking, tool runtime and idle time; the slim-regime figures have never been tried; the verification impact of the photo swap comes from text matching, not from running a single verifier; the runtimes of the retained checks were not measured; host limits are recollections. The composite schedule in this report stitches together estimates made under different scope assumptions and should be treated as an order of magnitude. Finally, the reposts measure bookmarks rather than use, about 60 video posts were not played, and nobody has data on which INTRX components visitors view or copy.

## Conclusion

The regime that slowed INTRX was verifying the wrong thing. It proved, to the byte, that 520 components match a set of Figma tiles and original controllers, and the audits show those tiles are the weak layer; the same discipline pointed at a poster contact sheet and a deployed URL would have cost a fraction and found the real defects. That is why the critical path runs through a decision record rather than through any technical task: the five workstreams examined here (polish, photos, launch, extension, code reuse) are all blocked by the same handful of rules, and each week those rules stand costs more than any step in the plan.

The second implication concerns identity. Engineering, motion and accessibility are already ahead of the libraries the owner admires, and the palette is common currency in 2026, so what will make INTRX look authored is light, material, real content and one coherent image world, exactly the layers the previous process never measured. Borrowing permissively licensed code will speed up coverage, but the catalog's defensible position remains the thing nobody else in that cohort offers: framework-free, single-file, prompt-first components at scale. Ports and dependencies should be labelled as guests in that house rather than allowed to redefine it.
